The Real Test
Interview Preparation
Big tech interviews have a formula. Learn the formula.
📞 Phone Screen Round
  • 30-45 minutes with a recruiter
  • General background questions
  • Why this company?
  • Walk through your resume
  • 1 easy coding problem sometimes
  • Prepare 60-second self-intro
💻 Technical Screen Round
  • 45-60 minutes on shared editor
  • 1-2 Medium LeetCode problems
  • Think aloud the entire time
  • Clarify before you code
  • Test with examples after coding
  • Discuss time/space complexity
🏢 Virtual Onsite (3-5 Rounds)
  • 2-3 coding rounds (Medium/Hard)
  • 1 system design round (junior: lighter)
  • 1 behavioural round (STAR)
  • Full day — stay energised
  • Ask good questions at end of each round
🌟 STAR Method (Behavioural)
  • Situation — set the scene briefly
  • Task — what was your responsibility
  • Action — what YOU specifically did
  • Result — measurable outcome
  • Prepare 10 stories covering different situations
  • Use your Flutter projects as examples
🗣️ Questions to prepare answers for
  • Tell me about yourself
  • Why do you want to work here?
  • Tell me about a challenging project
  • Describe a time you failed
  • Tell me about a conflict with a teammate
  • What is your greatest achievement?
  • How do you handle tight deadlines?
  • Tell me about a time you showed leadership
  • What are your strengths and weaknesses?
  • Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
Getting In
Application Process
How to find, apply, and follow up on job openings the smart way.
  • 1
    Build your application profile first
    Before applying anywhere — clean up your GitHub (every project needs a README), update LinkedIn with all projects and skills, and prepare a 1-page ATS-friendly CV. You already have a strong Flutter CV from our sessions — use that.
  • 2
    Find the job opening
    Check the company careers page directly (careers.google.com, metacareers.com, careers.microsoft.com). Also check LinkedIn Jobs, Glassdoor, and Levels.fyi for roles. Set up email alerts so new roles land in your inbox the day they open.
  • 3
    Get a referral if possible
    Search LinkedIn for people who work at your target company. Send a short, respectful message asking if they would refer you. A referral increases your chance of getting an interview by 5-10x. Most people are willing to help if you are polite and prepared.
  • 4
    Tailor your application for each role
    Read the job description carefully. Use keywords from it in your CV and cover email. If they mention Kotlin — put Kotlin. If they mention cross-platform — highlight Flutter and CraftyBay. ATS systems scan for keywords before a human sees your CV.
  • 5
    Follow up after applying
    If you hear nothing after 1-2 weeks, send one polite follow-up email or LinkedIn message to the recruiter. Keep it short: "I applied for [role] on [date] and remain very interested. I wanted to check if my application was received." One follow-up is professional. Two is too many.
  • 6
    Negotiate the offer
    When you get an offer — never accept immediately. Say "Thank you, I am excited. Can I have a few days to review?" Then research market rates on Levels.fyi and Glassdoor, and ask for more. Base salary, equity, signing bonus, and remote options are all negotiable. The worst they can say is no.
